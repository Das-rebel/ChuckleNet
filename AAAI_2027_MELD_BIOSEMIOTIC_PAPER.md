# HISTORICAL / SUPERSEDED — AAAI 2027 MELD Biosemiotic Paper

**Original date:** 2026-04-05  
**Status:** HISTORICAL — RETAINED FOR PROVENANCE

> This document is intentionally preserved because it records an earlier research hypothesis. It is **not submission-ready** and must not be cited as the current ChuckleNet position.

## Why this document is superseded

The original draft treated MELD `joy` labels as evidence for laughter and, further, as a proxy for authentic/Duchenne laughter. That inference is not justified by the dataset. It also framed the work as text-primary, whereas the current ChuckleNet program explicitly does **not** compete in text humor/emotion classification.

The original numerical claims, including the reported 87.5% accuracy, are therefore historical results of an earlier analysis and are not current evidence for laughter detection, Duchenne laughter, or multimodal interaction intelligence.

## What is retained

The document remains useful for tracing the origin of three ideas:

1. laughter as a social/interactional signal;
2. biosemiotics as a theoretical lens for interpreting behavior as a sign;
3. the need to consider temporal and relational context rather than isolated events.

Those ideas survive, but their empirical implementation has changed.

## Corrected empirical stance

MELD may be used for exploratory multimodal alignment or controlled semantic/context baselines. It must not be used to claim that `joy = laughter`, `joy = authentic laughter`, or `joy = Duchenne laughter`.

A future biosemiotic study should instead formulate independently testable hypotheses about how observable vocal/visual events acquire interactional meaning from producer, recipient, timing, antecedent and response.
